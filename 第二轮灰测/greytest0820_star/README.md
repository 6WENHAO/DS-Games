# ARMOUR — a multi-tank simulator with interactive interiors

A tank simulator that runs in a browser with **no build step, no dependencies and no
asset files**. Five very different tanks, each with a walk-in crew compartment whose
switches, levers, handwheels, breech, ammunition racks and optics all actually do
something. Everything — the 3D renderer, the terrain, the vehicle models, the
interiors and the sound — is generated by code at runtime.

```
open index.html          (double-click is enough — file:// works)
```

Then pick a tank in the garage, press **DEPLOY**, and the built-in tutorial takes you
from a cold, dead vehicle to a first hit on the range.

**English / 简体中文** — the interface, every control label, all crew dialogue and the
whole tutorial are bilingual. The language follows your browser on first run; the
**EN / 中文** buttons in the garage (or the small button top-right inside the tank)
switch it live, and the choice is remembered.

---

## Interactive tutorial

Nineteen steps, each validated against real simulator state — the only way past a step is
to actually do it. The panel names the control it wants, the compartment **pulses a ring
on that exact switch**, and when it is behind you an arrow at the edge of the screen
points the way.

Battery → fuel cock → starter → parking brake → gear → drive → gunner's seat → breech →
load → arm the circuit → sight → range → fire → hit → commander's cupola.

The step list adapts to the vehicle: an autoloading T-72 never asks you to open the
breech by hand, and a tank without a laser rangefinder teaches the range drum instead of
lasing. **F2** starts or hides it at any time; the garage has a TUTORIAL button and an
auto-start checkbox.

## The five vehicles

| Tank | Nation / year | Crew | Gun | What makes it feel different |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M4A3(75) Sherman** | US 1943 | 5 | 75 mm M3 | Roomy, well-lit compartment, hydraulic traverse, bow MG station, best ergonomics of its generation |
| **T-34-85** | USSR 1944 | 5 | 85 mm ZiS-S-53 | Cramped turret, slow reload, floor ammo bins, manual traverse handwheel as the backup |
| **Tiger I** | Germany 1942 | 5 | 8.8 cm KwK 36 | Steering *wheel* and pre-selector gearbox, binocular TZF sight, foot-pedal hydraulic traverse, glacial 9°/s turret |
| **T-72B3** | Russia 2013 | 3 | 125 mm 2A46M-5 | 22-round carousel autoloader under the floor, Sosna-U thermal sight, laser rangefinder, no loader — the commander doubles up |
| **M1A2 SEP Abrams** | US 1999 | 4 | 120 mm M256 | Gas turbine, T-bar yoke, fire-control computer, CITV hunter-killer thermal, blast-door ammunition |

Each has its own hull, turret, running gear, interior layout, instrument set and
handling. The garage screen shows the full stat card and a live turntable.

## Crew stations

Press **1 2 3 4** to physically move between seats. The station you take decides what
you can see and reach:

* **Driver** (hull) — master switch, fuel cock, starter, gear lever, tillers/wheel/yoke,
  pedals, parking brake, gauges (rpm, speed, fuel, coolant, oil), lamps, periscope,
  hatch, extinguisher, intercom, bilge pump.
* **Gunner** (turret) — telescopic/thermal sight, traverse grip with the firing trigger,
  elevation handwheel, manual traverse wheel, turret power, power/manual selector,
  firing-circuit safety, range drum, azimuth indicator, magnification, coax MG, plus
  laser rangefinder / stabiliser / ballistic computer on the modern tanks.
* **Loader** (turret) — breech operating lever, rammer, AP/HE/HEAT/Smoke racks (the
  brass in the racks physically disappears as you shoot), spent-case bag, hatch,
  extinguisher. The Abrams loader also has the knee switch for the blast door.
* **Commander** (turret) — cupola vision blocks, hatch, radio, traverse override,
  hunter-killer target designation, smoke launchers, map board, pintle MG.

Because the turret interior is parented to the turret ring, **traversing the gun swings
the whole fighting compartment around the driver** — exactly as it does in a real tank.

## Views

**V** cycles: interior → gunner's sight → periscope → head out of the hatch → external
orbit. The sight has a proper aperture mask, a period-appropriate reticle (chevron plate
for WWII, digital box for modern), a super-elevation aiming mark for the range you have
set or lased, and thermal / night channels where the vehicle has them.

## Controls

The tutorial teaches all of this in order; this table is the reference.

| | |
|---|---|
| **1 2 3 4** | take the driver / gunner / loader / commander station |
| **V** | cycle view · **O** labels · **Tab** garage · **F1** help · **F2** tutorial |
| **Left drag** | look around (or orbit / lay the gun in the sight) |
| **Left click** | operate the control under the cursor · **Right click** alternate action |
| **M / N / I** | master switch / fuel cock / starter |
| **Shift / Ctrl / P** | gear up / gear down / parking brake |
| **W A S D** | throttle, steer, brake |
| **Q E / R F** | traverse / elevate |
| **Space** | fire · **C** coax MG · **X** smoke |
| **Z / B / G / K** | select round / breech / load / arm firing circuit |
| **L / [ ] / Y / T** | lase / range drum / magnification / thermal-night |
| **H** | hatch at this station |

### Starting from cold

`M` master → `N` fuel cock → `I` starter → `P` release brake → `Shift` into gear → `W`.
The interlocks are real: no battery, no start; fuel cock shut, it just coughs; empty
gun or a safe firing circuit and the trigger does nothing. The HUD hint line walks you
through it, and every one of those steps is also a clickable switch in the cab.

## What is actually simulated

* Engine: battery/fuel-cock/starter chain, cranking, idle and load-dependent rpm, fuel
  burn (the turbine drinks 2.4× as much), coolant and oil pressure.
* Drivetrain: gears with per-gear top speed, rolling resistance, slope drag, brake and
  parking brake, tracked steering with speed-dependent rate and neutral steer/pivot for
  the vehicles that can do it.
* Terrain-following attitude: pitch and roll sampled under the hull, so the gun and the
  whole interior tilt with the ground; dust off the tracks.
* Gun: breech state, loading (manual ram vs autoloader cycle), rounds by type with
  separate counts, spent cases, recoil animation, dispersion that gets much worse when
  moving without a stabiliser, super-elevation ballistics with drag, penetration versus
  target armour with range fall-off, and non-penetrating hits.
* Turret: powered vs manual traverse rates per vehicle, elevation limits, commander's
  override slew, stabiliser and fire-control computer.
* Optics: magnification steps, day/night/thermal palettes, laser rangefinder, manual
  range drum.
* Damage-ish: fires you must put out with the extinguisher, hull HP, ammunition that
  runs out.
* Audio: the engine is a synthesised oscillator stack driven by rpm and load (with a
  turbine whine for the Abrams), the gun is a noise burst plus a descending thump, and
  the compartment low-passes everything when you are buttoned up.

## The gunnery range

Spawn is a motor pool with a hangar, watchtower, berms and three other tanks parked so
you can walk the camera around them. The range runs north with 100 m marker flags,
close-in panel targets and seven target vehicles from 210 m to 700 m, including one
that drives back and forth at 5 m/s so you have to learn to lead. Hits report
penetration and range; kills brew up and smoke for a while.

## Layout

```
index.html            page shell + HUD markup
css/style.css         instrument-panel skin
js/math3d.js          vectors, row-major 4x4 matrices, value noise, colour helpers
js/i18n.js            English / 简体中文 dictionary and the L.s / L.m helpers
js/tutorial.js        the 19 step trainer, validated against live simulator state
js/mesh.js            MeshBuilder: boxes, extrusions, tubes, domes, rings, plates
js/renderer.js        software 3D renderer on a 2D canvas: painter's algorithm,
                      near-plane clipping, flat shading, fog, day/thermal/night palettes
js/tanks.js           the five vehicle specs + parametric exterior models + LOD proxies
js/interiors.js       procedural control widgets, compartment shells, five interiors,
                      auto-facing and line-of-sight placement of every control
js/sim.js             TankSim (all the systems above), projectiles, particles
js/world.js           terrain with two LOD levels, scenery, range targets
js/audio.js           WebAudio synthesis, no samples
js/ui.js              garage, HUD readouts, log, tooltips
js/main.js            input, cameras, interior/exterior draw passes, overlays, loop
tests/                headless test suite (see below)
```

### Two design details worth knowing

**Controls are placed, then corrected.** Interiors are authored as a list of control
positions. At build time each control is (a) rotated to face the eye point of the
crewman who operates it and (b) ray-tested against the compartment geometry and slid
along the line of sight until it clears whatever was in front of it. Hand-authoring a
yaw/pitch pair for two hundred switches — and re-authoring them every time a gearbox
tunnel moves — is not worth anyone's time.

**Widgets are drawn with a depth bias.** A painter's-algorithm renderer sorts by
centroid depth, so a wide wall panel can otherwise be painted over the switch bolted to
it. Every widget is biased towards the camera by 22 cm.

## Tests

The whole game boots under node against a stubbed DOM, so it can be tested without a
browser:

```
node tests/smoke.js        # 70 checks: builds, deploys and flies all 5 tanks,
                           #   plus translation coverage and a full tutorial run
node tests/visual.js       # 28 checks: renders frames and analyses the pixels
node tests/stress.js       # 2100 frames of random view/station churn: no frozen frames
node tests/occlusion.js [tank] [station]   # diagnostic: what is blocking a control
node tests/render_png.js   # writes 20 reference PNGs to tests/out/
```

* **smoke.js** builds every model and interior, deploys each tank, starts the engine and
  drives it, renders every crew station in every view, operates *every* control
  (`act`, `act2`, `text`, `val`), loads and fires, resolves a hit on a target, checks
  crew ergonomics (eye inside the compartment, controls in reach and in view), drives
  the cursor onto a switch and clicks it, verifies the interlocks and map bounds, proves
  that **every** label / role / class / description / note has a Chinese translation,
  switches language mid-mission, and plays the tutorial through to the end.
* **visual.js** contains a small software rasteriser (`tests/raster.js`: scanline
  polygon fill, gradients, strokes, PNG writer) so the real renderer can be pointed at
  a pixel buffer offline. It then asserts things like "the sky is brighter than the
  ground", "the compartment is not an empty black frame", "the compartment lamp
  measurably brightens the interior", "the sight has a masked rim and a reticle across
  the centre", "the thermal channel repaints the picture", "the orbit camera frames the
  tank", "the tutorial pulses a ring on the control it is asking for and an arrow when
  it is off screen", and — by rendering each interior twice, once with the widgets
  suppressed — that **every control is really drawn where the player will click it**.
* **stress.js** throws 2100 frames of randomised station/view/key churn at the game and
  fails on any exception or any frame that comes back as a single flat colour.

All suites exit non-zero on failure.

## Fixed: the "screen turns one colour" bug

Switching views could occasionally freeze the picture as one flat colour. The cause was
`Renderer.line()` (gauge needles and tracers): it clipped a segment against the near
plane by lerping in **world** space and re-projecting, and when the clipped endpoint
landed a hair behind the plane again it recursed — forever. The resulting stack overflow
escaped the animation-frame callback, so the loop never re-scheduled and the flat
background that `begin()` had just painted stayed frozen on screen.

Two fixes, both covered by `tests/stress.js`:

1. `Renderer.line()` now clips in camera space in a single pass, with a NaN guard.
2. The frame body is wrapped so a throw can never stop the loop: the error is logged
   once, and if one view keeps failing the game recovers to the external view instead of
   freezing.

## Known limitations

* The renderer is a painter's-algorithm rasteriser on a 2D canvas: no per-pixel depth,
  so deeply interpenetrating geometry can sort oddly at extreme angles.
* Targets do not shoot back — this is a gunnery range, not a battle.
* Interior geometry is stylised: the compartments are correct in layout, ergonomics and
  control function, not in rivet-for-rivet dimensions.
* Text inside the 3D view is drawn as 2D overlay labels, so it does not tilt with the
  panels.
